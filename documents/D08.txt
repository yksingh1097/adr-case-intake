1. PURPOSE OF THIS REPORT

This report describes the clinical course of a patient with rheumatoid arthritis who developed severe bone marrow suppression during treatment with low-dose weekly methotrexate. It is submitted to the marketing authorisation holder and to the national pharmacovigilance centre in accordance with local reporting requirements. All patient identifiers other than initials have been removed.

4. SUSPECT MEDICINE

Methotrexate 2.5 mg tablets (oral).

Methotrexate was started by the rheumatology service on 7 October 2025 at a dose of 15 mg orally once weekly, taken on Tuesdays, together with folic acid 5 mg orally once weekly on Thursdays. The patient was counselled verbally and in writing on the once-weekly schedule and on the warning signs of toxicity.

Monitoring blood tests were performed every four weeks. Results were unremarkable through to February 2026 (see Table 2 on page 4).

At the rheumatology clinic visit on 2 March 2026 the patient reported persistent synovitis of both wrists and the right knee (DAS28-CRP 4.9). The treating rheumatologist increased the dose of methotrexate to 20 mg orally once weekly from that date. Folic acid was continued unchanged. A repeat blood test was requested for four weeks later.

The patient confirmed, on direct questioning after admission, that she had taken methotrexate strictly once weekly at the prescribed dose and had not taken any extra tablets.

6. CLINICAL COURSE

6.1 Presentation

On 24 March 2026 the patient presented to the emergency department with a five-day history of painful mouth ulcers, difficulty swallowing, fatigue and easy bruising on the forearms. Her daughter reported that the patient had become increasingly lethargic over the preceding week. She had taken her weekly methotrexate dose on 17 March 2026 as scheduled.

On examination: temperature 38.9 degrees Celsius, heart rate 112 beats per minute, blood pressure 98/56 mmHg, respiratory rate 22 breaths per minute, oxygen saturation 95 percent on room air. Extensive oral mucositis with confluent ulceration of the buccal mucosa, tongue and soft palate. Scattered ecchymoses on both forearms and petechiae on the lower legs. No focal source of infection identified on examination. Chest clear.

6.2 Initial investigations

Full blood count on arrival: haemoglobin 82 g/L, white cell count 0.6 x 10^9/L, absolute neutrophil count 0.1 x 10^9/L, platelet count 14 x 10^9/L. Blood film: pancytopenia, no blasts, no dysplastic features.

Renal function: creatinine 168 micromol/L (baseline 104), estimated glomerular filtration rate 27 mL/min/1.73 m2. Urea 14.2 mmol/L. Sodium 134 mmol/L, potassium 4.6 mmol/L.

Liver function: ALT 68 U/L, AST 54 U/L, bilirubin 19 micromol/L, albumin 29 g/L.

C-reactive protein 212 mg/L. Lactate 2.8 mmol/L. Procalcitonin 9.4 micrograms/L.

Serum methotrexate concentration (drawn approximately 7 days after the last dose): 0.09 micromol/L, which the laboratory reported as above the expected level for the time elapsed since a low oral dose.

Blood cultures, urine culture and a swab of the oral ulcers were sent. Chest radiograph: no consolidation.

6.3 Working diagnosis

Methotrexate toxicity with pancytopenia, severe oral mucositis and neutropenic sepsis, probably precipitated by the recent dose increase in the setting of acute-on-chronic kidney impairment with reduced methotrexate clearance.

6.4 Management

The patient was admitted to the haematology ward under joint rheumatology and haematology care.

Methotrexate was discontinued permanently on 24 March 2026.

Intravenous folinic acid (calcium folinate) rescue was commenced in the emergency department at 15 mg every six hours and continued for 72 hours.

Empirical intravenous piperacillin-tazobactam was started within one hour of arrival after blood cultures were drawn, in line with the hospital's neutropenic sepsis pathway. Vancomycin was added on day 2 when blood cultures grew a Gram-positive coccus, later identified as viridans group Streptococcus, consistent with an oral mucosal source.

Filgrastim 300 micrograms subcutaneously once daily was commenced on 24 March 2026 on the advice of haematology.

Supportive care included intravenous fluids, platelet transfusion on 25 and 26 March 2026 (platelet count below 10 x 10^9/L), two units of packed red cells on 26 March 2026, mouth care with chlorhexidine and lidocaine mouthwash, and total parenteral nutrition from 26 March 2026 because of inability to swallow.

Omeprazole was changed to intravenous pantoprazole. Cilazapril was withheld because of hypotension and acute kidney injury. Paracetamol was continued.

6.5 Progress

Days 1 to 3 (24 to 26 March 2026): The patient remained febrile despite antibiotics. Hypotension responded to intravenous fluids initially. Mucositis worsened; she was unable to take anything by mouth.

Day 4 (27 March 2026): The neutrophil count showed early recovery (0.1 x 10^9/L). Fever persisted. Repeat blood cultures from 26 March 2026 were negative.

Day 5 (28 March 2026): Neutrophil count 0.3 x 10^9/L. The patient developed increasing shortness of breath. Oxygen saturation fell to 88 percent on room air; she was started on high-flow nasal oxygen. Chest radiograph showed new bilateral lower zone infiltrates. A CT scan of the chest was consistent with bilateral pneumonia; no pulmonary embolus was seen. Antibiotics were broadened to meropenem. The intensive care team reviewed the patient; after discussion with the patient and her family, and in keeping with her previously documented wishes, a decision was made not to escalate to invasive ventilation.

Day 6 (29 March 2026): The patient deteriorated further overnight with worsening hypoxaemia and hypotension not responsive to fluids. The neutrophil count had risen to 0.6 x 10^9/L. A family meeting was held and the focus of care was changed to comfort.

Day 7 (30 March 2026): The patient died at 04:35 on 30 March 2026 with her family present.

7. OUTCOME

Fatal. The cause of death recorded on the death certificate was:
  1a. Bilateral pneumonia with septic shock
  1b. Neutropenia due to methotrexate toxicity
  2.  Rheumatoid arthritis; chronic kidney disease stage 3

The case was referred to the coroner, who accepted the clinical cause of death and did not require a post-mortem examination.

9. CAUSALITY ASSESSMENT

The temporal relationship between the dose increase of 2 March 2026 and the onset of mucositis on or about 19 March 2026 (five days before presentation), the pattern of pancytopenia with mucositis, the raised serum methotrexate concentration seven days after the last dose, the recovery of the neutrophil count following folinic acid rescue and filgrastim, and the absence of an alternative explanation all support a causal role for methotrexate. Acute-on-chronic kidney impairment reduced methotrexate clearance and is considered a contributing factor. The dose increase from 15 mg to 20 mg once weekly in a patient with an estimated glomerular filtration rate of about 50 mL/min/1.73 m2 is considered, in retrospect, to have carried a higher risk than appreciated at the time.

The treating team's assessment: methotrexate - probable. Death related to the adverse reaction: yes.

10. ADDITIONAL INFORMATION

No dispensing or administration error was identified on review of the community pharmacy record and the patient's own medication diary. The patient had taken a total of 24 weekly doses.

The case has been discussed at the hospital's morbidity and mortality meeting. Local actions include a review of the renal-dose guidance in the departmental methotrexate protocol and a change to the monitoring interval after any dose increase in patients with an estimated glomerular filtration rate below 60 mL/min/1.73 m2.
